Ternary Training Map

Training a model whose weights are only {−1, 0, +1}, with no full-precision copy of them (optimizer state at most sublinear in parameter count). This page lays out what we tried, why, where it ran, and what is still unexplained.

What the theory still can't explain

Open gaps

open

No integrator

Master's latent weights sum every small push and undo moves the gradient stops supporting (96% of its 100-step moves agree with the summed gradient). Ours can only flip or not; evidence below the flip threshold is lost. No fix so far replaces this.

open

Proposal quality

M's flip proposals score 0.48 precision against the true gradient, below one batch's gradient (0.63). Look-ahead filters them, but its precision does not improve with more batches, because it judges all flips jointly.

lead

Scale that can grow

Master's per-matrix scale grows; ours is fixed at β = 1/√(K·ρ), so attention can't sharpen. The additive adapter supplies scale but, unconstrained, turns into noise. Weight decay on it fixed the toy. Untested on text.

lead

Flip rate vs structure

Peak rate 0.02 was tuned for loss. In the toy, the same optimizer builds induction only once flips are rare enough (rate decay or ÷4). Text runs may churn fine circuits too fast.

lead

Why weight decay works

Weight decay on both A and B equals a nuclear-norm penalty on A·Bᵀ: directions without steady gradient die. Measured: adapter effective rank 2.5 with decay vs 3.0 without; A only 30% smaller. One seed; needs repeats.

open

The 12M bend

After ~12M tokens master and fp32 steepen to slope −0.30; ours stays at −0.21. Candidate cause: the long-context circuits we never build. Not shown directly.
